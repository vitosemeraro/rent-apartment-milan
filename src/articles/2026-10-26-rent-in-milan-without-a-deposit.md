Looking to **rent an apartment in Milan with no deposit**? It is possible, but you need to know what "no deposit" really means and what to check before you pay anything. This guide explains the usual Milan rules, what changes with a direct booking, and which flats fit a stay of one month or more.

## What is the usual deposit in Milan?

Italian law allows a deposit of up to **three months' rent** on a standard lease. In Milan landlords and agencies often ask for the full amount. Add an agency fee of about one month plus VAT, and the up-front cost is high.

Example for a studio at €1,500 per month:

| Up-front cost | Typical Milan rental | RAM apartments |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deposit | €3,000–4,500 | No three-month deposit |
| Agency fee | about €1,800 | None: you book directly |
| Bills and Wi-Fi activation | €100–200 | Included in the price |
| First month | €1,500 | First month |

The figures for a typical rental are market estimates, not a quote. What you pay with us is shown in the booking calendar.

## Can you really rent with no deposit?

For monthly stays in our apartments, **we do not ask for the usual three-month deposit**. Payment terms are written in the contract, so read it before you sign. This is what you should check in any offer:

- Is a deposit requested, and how much?
- Is the lease registered with the Agenzia delle Entrate?
- Are electricity, gas, heating, water and Wi-Fi included?
- Do you get a receipt for every payment?
- Do you know who the host is, and have you seen real photos?

Never send money to someone you have not identified. A scam often starts with a "no deposit" flat at a price that is too good to be true.

## Is a monthly lease valid for visa and residence?

Yes. For stays over 30 days we sign a transitional or student lease and register it. Read how it works in our guide to the [transitional lease in Milan](/transitional-lease-contract-milan/). The tourist tax applies only to stays under 30 nights, so it is not due with a monthly contract.
