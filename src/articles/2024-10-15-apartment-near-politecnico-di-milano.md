Politecnico di Milano is one of Europe's top universities for engineering, architecture and design, and many of its programmes are taught in English. Finding a room or a flat near campus is the first challenge for international students: this guide explains where to look, what it costs and how to avoid the usual traps.

## Where the Politecnico campuses are

- **Leonardo (Città Studi)**: the main campus, around Piazza Leonardo da Vinci, served by the M2 green line (Piola and Lambrate stations).
- **Bovisa**: engineering and design schools in the north-west, served by the suburban railway (Milano Bovisa station).

Living right next to campus is convenient but expensive. Many students choose a well-connected area a few metro stops away and save several hundred euros a month.

## The M1 + M2 route from Precotto and Turro

Our apartments are in north-east Milan on the **M1 red line**. From Turro or Precotto you ride three or four stops to **Loreto**, change to the **M2** and get off at **Piola**: about **15–20 minutes** door to campus. It's one of the fastest commutes to Città Studi you can get outside the university district itself.

## What students usually struggle with

- **High deposits**: many landlords ask for 2–3 months up front, plus an agency fee.
- **Hidden costs**: electricity, gas, heating and Wi-Fi often come on top of the rent.
- **Contracts in Italian**: a lease you don't understand is a risk, and an unregistered one is useless for residence permits and scholarships.

## When to start looking

Start **6–8 weeks before** the semester begins. September and February are the busiest months: if you are coming for the autumn semester, book by July.
