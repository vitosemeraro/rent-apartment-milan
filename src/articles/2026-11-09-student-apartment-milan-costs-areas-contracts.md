## Which lease do I need as a student?

For stays over 30 days we sign a **transitional or student lease** and register it with the Agenzia delle Entrate. It is valid for residence and permit purposes. Our guide to the [transitional lease in Milan](/transitional-lease-contract-milan/) explains how it works.

The Milan tourist tax applies only to stays under 30 nights, so it is not due with a monthly contract.

## Is there a deposit?

For monthly stays in our apartments, **we do not ask for the usual deposit of up to three months' rent**. Payment terms are written in the contract, so read it before you sign. See the page on [apartments in Milan with no deposit](/apartments-milan-no-deposit/).

Check these points in any offer in Milan:

- Is a deposit requested, and how much?
- Is the lease registered with the Agenzia delle Entrate?
- Are electricity, gas, heating, water and Wi-Fi included?
- Do you get a receipt for every payment?
- Do you know the host, and have you seen real photos?

Never send money to someone you have not identified. Scams often start with a room at a price that is too good to be true.

## Whole apartment or a room in a shared flat?

A room is often cheaper per person, but you share kitchen and bathroom and rarely get your own desk. A whole apartment costs more and gives you a quiet place to study, your own kitchen and a washing machine. Both flats have a work area. The one in Via Val di Non also has gigabit fibre and a balcony.

## What documents do I need?

1. Passport or ID card.
2. The Italian tax code (**codice fiscale**), from the Agenzia delle Entrate or your consulate.
3. Proof of the reason for your stay, such as your university enrolment.
4. For a visa or residence: the registered lease.
