Milan attracts students from all over the world: Bocconi for economics, Politecnico for engineering and design, Statale and Bicocca for sciences and humanities, Brera and NABA for the arts. Choosing where to live shapes your whole experience.

## Pick the area by commute, not by fame

A central address is expensive and often noisy. A quiet street a few minutes from a metro station can give you more space for less money. From **Precotto and Turro on the M1**, Bicocca is about 15 minutes away, Politecnico about 20 and the Duomo about 20.

## What students need at home

- A **real desk** and fast Wi-Fi for study sessions and online exams.
- A **kitchen**: eating out every day in Milan adds up quickly.
- A **washing machine** and enough storage.
- **Quiet**: a residential street, not a nightlife district.

## All-inclusive rent

One monthly price with electricity, gas, heating, water and gigabit Wi-Fi means no bill surprises in winter, when heating costs peak.
